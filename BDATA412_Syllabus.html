Course Description

Provides advanced training on visualization methods to show data in complex and compelling ways. Applies complex visualization methods to examine topics including project management, the grammar of graphics, data types, data structure, modeling, complex and interactive data visualization, and data dashboards.

This course builds on the foundations from B DATA 200 and B DATA 232 to take students deep into the mathematical and computational machinery behind advanced data visualization. Students will master the linear algebra that underlies dimensionality reduction, learn to identify natural groupings in data through cluster analysis, and extract latent structures using eigenvalue decomposition, correspondence analysis, and neural-network-based embeddings. Throughout, students will translate theory into practice using Python in Google Colab, producing compelling interactive visualizations that communicate complex multivariate relationships.

The central question of this course is: how do we reveal and communicate hidden structure in high-dimensional data through visualization?

Course Objectives

  • Understand the mathematical foundations (linear algebra, distance metrics, eigenvalue decomposition) that power advanced visualization techniques.
  • Apply cluster analysis methods (K-Means, Hierarchical, DBSCAN) to discover natural groupings in multivariate data and visualize results effectively.
  • Implement dimensionality reduction techniques (PCA, Correspondence Analysis, MDS, t-SNE, UMAP, Autoencoders) to project high-dimensional data into interpretable low-dimensional visualizations.
  • Critically evaluate and select appropriate methods based on data characteristics, research questions, and audience needs.
  • Produce publication-quality interactive visualizations using Python libraries (matplotlib, plotnine, plotly, scikit-learn, NetworkX).
  • Communicate complex analytical results to both technical and non-technical audiences through data storytelling.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

  1. Represent data as vectors and matrices, and perform operations (dot product, norms, matrix multiplication, eigenvalue decomposition) relevant to data visualization.
  2. Compute and interpret covariance and correlation matrices, and explain the role of standardization in multivariate analysis.
  3. Apply and compare clustering algorithms (K-Means, Agglomerative, DBSCAN, t-SNE, UMAP), validate results using silhouette scores and elbow methods, and produce cluster-based visualizations.
  4. Perform PCA, Correspondence Analysis, MDS, and Autoencoders to reduce dimensionality and create biplots, factor maps, and embedding visualizations.
  5. Design and deliver an end-to-end data analysis project with advanced visualizations, from data preparation through presentation.
  6. Use LLM tools and prompt engineering effectively to support advanced data analysis and visualization workflows in Google Colab.

Course Schedule — 8 Sections

Sec. Weeks Topic Key Content & Google Colab Labs
1 Sep 29 – Oct 1 Curse of Dimensionality & Linear Algebra Foundations Distance concentration; sparsity; KNN breakdown; overfitting risk; vectors & matrices; scalar multiplication; vector addition/subtraction; dot product; norms (L1, L2, L∞); cosine similarity.
Lab: Implementing vector operations, distance computation, and KNN in Python.
2 Oct 6 – Oct 8 Matrix Operations & Data Geometry Matrix-vector & matrix-matrix multiplication; linear transformations (rotation, scaling, shear, projection); determinant; matrix inverse; covariance & correlation matrices; standardization (z-scores); correlation as distance.
Lab: Building covariance/correlation matrices from data; visualizing transformations; heatmaps.
3 Oct 13 – Oct 15 K-Means & Hierarchical Clustering What is a cluster; data matrix & distance matrix; Gram matrix trick; K-Means objective & Lloyd's algorithm; k-means++ initialization; matrix form; elbow method; agglomerative clustering; linkage types (single, complete, average, Ward); dendrograms; Lance–Williams formula; cophenetic correlation.
Lab: K-Means from scratch; scikit-learn clustering; dendrogram visualization; elbow plots.
4 Oct 20 – Oct 22 DBSCAN, t-SNE & UMAP Density-based clustering (core, border, noise points); DBSCAN algorithm; choosing ε (k-distance plot); non-convex shapes; t-SNE (affinities, KL divergence, perplexity); UMAP (local connectivity, fuzzy union, cross-entropy); cluster validation (silhouette, F-statistic); cluster profiling & personas.
Lab: DBSCAN on non-convex data; t-SNE & UMAP embeddings; interactive plotly visualizations; silhouette analysis.
5 Oct 27 – Oct 29 Principal Component Analysis (PCA) Eigenvectors & eigenvalues; geometric intuition of PCA; covariance eigen-decomposition; loadings & scores; variance explained; scree plot & Kaiser rule; biplots; PCA via SVD; correlation-based PCA.
Lab: PCA from scratch using eigenvalues; scikit-learn PCA; biplot construction; interactive variance-explained plots.
6 Nov 3 – Nov 5 Correspondence Analysis & MCA Chi-squared test for contingency tables; CA decomposition of Pearson χ²; profiles & inertia; SVD & coordinates; reading a CA map; contributions (CTR) & quality (cos²); Multiple Correspondence Analysis (MCA); indicator matrix; Burt matrix.
Lab: CA on contingency tables; MCA on survey data; factor maps; interactive correspondence plots.
7 Nov 10 – Nov 12 Multidimensional Scaling & Autoencoders Classical MDS (double-centering, Gram matrix, coordinates); stress & goodness-of-fit; metric vs non-metric MDS; MDS vs PCA; autoencoders (encoder/decoder architecture, bottleneck, reconstruction loss); forward pass & SGD; linear AE ≡ PCA; nonlinear AE advantages.
Lab: MDS from distance matrices; stress computation; building a simple autoencoder in PyTorch/TensorFlow; embedding visualization.
8 Nov 17 – Dec 3 Applied Project & Presentations Integration of all methods; project work sessions; method selection framework; peer feedback; in-person presentations (last week: Dec 1 & Dec 3).
Lab: Full end-to-end project in Google Colab combining clustering + dimensionality reduction + interactive visualization.

Assignment Details

Online Exercises (20%)

Weekly assignments completed in Google Colab that reinforce the concepts from each section. Exercises include implementing algorithms from scratch (e.g., KNN, K-Means, PCA), computing matrix operations, generating visualizations, and interpreting results. Exercises are due by the start of the following week's class and are graded for correctness and code quality.

  • Submitted as shared Google Colab links on Canvas.
  • Each exercise is worth equal weight; the lowest score is dropped.
  • Collaboration is encouraged but each student must submit their own notebook.

Exams (20% each × 3 = 60% total)

Three exams assess conceptual understanding and computational skills. Exams are in-class on Thursdays during regular class time (1:15 – 3:15 PM). Each exam covers the material from its designated sections:

Exam 1 (Thu, Oct 15): Curse of dimensionality, vectors, matrices, norms, dot product, cosine similarity, matrix operations, determinant, inverse, covariance & correlation matrices, standardization.

Exam 2 (Thu, Oct 29): K-Means (objective, Lloyd's algorithm, matrix form), hierarchical clustering (linkage, dendrograms), DBSCAN, t-SNE, UMAP, cluster validation (silhouette, elbow, F-statistic).

Exam 3 (Thu, Nov 12): PCA (eigenvalues, eigenvectors, loadings, scores, SVD), Correspondence Analysis (χ², inertia, profiles), MCA, MDS (double-centering, stress), autoencoders (forward pass, loss, linear AE ≡ PCA).

Exams include both analytical problems (hand computation) and Python coding questions.

Applied Project — Google Colab + In-Person Presentation (20%)

A comprehensive data analysis project completed individually or in groups of up to 3 students. The project demonstrates mastery of the course material by applying multiple techniques to a real-world dataset. Components:

Google Colab Notebook (10%)

A polished, reproducible notebook that includes: data description and preprocessing, at least one clustering method, at least one dimensionality reduction technique, interactive visualizations (plotly), and a written interpretation of findings. Due: November 29.

In-Person Presentation (10%)

A 10–15 minute in-person presentation during the last week of class (December 1 or December 3). All group members must present. The presentation should communicate the problem, methods, key visualizations, and insights to a mixed audience. Attendance at both presentation days is mandatory.
